Measured over the whole thirty-six-entry `gates` array, it aggregates twenty-one verification contracts, forty-eight requirements, and thirty-seven work-order-keyed evidence paths (thirty-six existing plus one `WO-RLS-011` retains). ## Why the contract was re-issued three times

`REL-SEH-012` was approved at 2026-08-25T10:28:10Z. `WO-AEX-005` reached `implemented` at 2026-08-25T10:29:40Z, ninety seconds later, and its authorized bytes are inside the packaged surface, so the approved allow-list stopped describing the release unit almost immediately. An approved allow-list is not widened in place. The 0.6.0 history is the precedent: `REL-SEH-008`, `REL-SEH-009`, and `REL-SEH-010` were each rejected and re-issued as that unit grew from nine to fourteen gates under `REL-SEH-011`. The release owner therefore rejected `REL-SEH-012` and issued `REL-SEH-013` with the thirty-four-gate unit, which the owner approved.

The second succession is about the work order, not the unit. `WO-RLS-009`'s aggregate scope had been amended to match the new unit, but the owner never approved that amendment, and the governing evaluator refuses an `approved` to `approved` transition (`WEX201`), so no re-approval event could be recorded on it. Shown three measured routes, the owner chose to reject `WO-RLS-009` and issue `WO-RLS-010`. Because `REL-SEH-013` named `WO-RLS-009` in `gates`, that left an approved contract naming a rejected member, and an approved contract is not repaired in place either — so `REL-SEH-013` and `WO-RLS-009` were rejected in one atomic transaction at 2026-08-25T11:47:44Z and re-issued as `REL-SEH-014` and `WO-RLS-010`, approved together at 2026-08-25T11:53:28Z.

The third succession repeated the first, faster. `WO-ADS-002` reached `implemented` at 2026-08-25T11:54:14Z — forty-six seconds after that approval — and with `WO-ADS-001`, implemented at 2026-08-25T11:15:03Z, both reached `main` through the true merges of pull requests #152 and #153 at `701e456794636e83ff78eb9910df55dfc1eedd9c`. Both change `se_harness/` runtime modules and distributed managed templates, so both belong in the unit. Rather than pay a fourth succession, the owner changed the sequencing: `REL-SEH-014` and `WO-RLS-010` were rejected, `REL-SEH-015` was issued carrying the thirty-six-gate unit in `draft`, and `WO-RLS-011` defers its aggregate census to whatever the contract names at its approval. The owner then accepted `REL-SEH-015` the same day, so the contract's `gates` is fixed and only `WO-RLS-011`'s deferred census still absorbs a further landing without a succession.

Two properties keep the cascade bounded. `WO-RLS-011` declares `REQ-DST-006`, `SPEC-DST-001`, `ARCH-DST-001`, `ADR-DST-001`, and `VER-DST-001` rather than a release contract, so a later contract succession breaks no graph edge pointing at it. And its prose fixes no census, so a corrected `gates` array does not contradict approved work-order text. No work was started under any rejected artifact: no start preflight, no version move, no build, no candidate commit.
